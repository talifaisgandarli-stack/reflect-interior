# Professional Concept Moodboard Methodology (hospitality / patisserie / boutique), applied to SPRING Cake Boutique, Baku

Scope note: about 20 tool calls were used (searches plus scrapes). Several fetches failed on DNS (WebFetch), so some pages were read through Firecrawl instead. Published, step-by-step "concept deck" templates from the named elite studios (Ashby, Sella, Child, Dimore, Mahdavi, Joyce Wang, Masquespacio, Yod, Linehouse) are **not publicly available**. What *is* published are their stated process principles, finished-project palettes, and Kelly Wearstler's "vibe tray" method. Layout rules come from architecture and interior education and presentation guides. Treat the studio-specific "board structure" below as **inferred** unless a citation is given.

---

## 1. What board structure do top studios use in concept presentations?

### Takeaway
None of the named studios publishes its deck template. The common published pattern is this: start from the client/brand story and the emotion the space should evoke, then build a material-led palette from physical samples, then resolve lighting, FF&E and details. Kelly Wearstler is the best-documented case. Her studio runs programming, then architectural plans, then mood boards, and a physical "material/vibe tray" is the first design artefact.

### Cited Findings
- Kelly Wearstler: her studio's conceptual design phase is "where the programming, architectural plans, and mood boards come together" (Malibu Diaries Part 2, Conceptual Design) — [Wearstler World Substack](https://wearstlerworld.substack.com/p/the-return-to-malibu-part-2-conceptual)
- Wearstler's process begins with material-led exploration drawing on a large studio archive of samples. She "pull[s] together a material mood board, gathering textures, colour palettes and artworks into a central repository" (the "material tray") — [The Local Project profile](https://thelocalproject.com.au/articles/profile-kelly-wearstler-issue-17-feature-the-local-project/)
- Wearstler's 5 vibe-tray tips: keep it flexible (one tray per room, nothing permanent); mix material families (stone, metal, wood, plaster, fabric); see and touch materials in person; create synergy between materials; use texture deliberately — [MasterClass](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/kelly-wearstlers-tips-for-creating-a-vibe-tray)
- Sella Concept (London): every project begins with an in-depth understanding of the space and defining the emotions the environment should evoke. The concept then evolves in dialogue with clients and master fabricators. Its material language includes wicker, terrazzo, timber panelling, geometric forms and de Chirico-like colour. For the Clay Table, the material and colour were recreated with a Cornish clay specialist to match Marrakech's "faded palette" — [Elle Decoration UK](https://www.elledecoration.co.uk/decorating/a30533982/sella-concept-interior-designer-profile/); [Clay Works](https://clay-works.com/sella-concept-clay-table/)
- Studio Ashby (Sophie Ashby) often starts a scheme from a piece of art and pulls the palette from it (e.g. "silvery blues, warm grays and rosy pinks from the client's own collection of paintings"). She wants an "element of surprise" rather than matchy-matchy schemes and uses a natural palette of materials (e.g. burr elm replacing grey-painted joinery) — [Living Etc interview](https://www.livingetc.com/features/sophie-ashby-interview); [Living Etc lessons](https://www.livingetc.com/whats-news/studio-ashby-interior-design-lessons-222453)
- Masquespacio: the process starts with the client's history and aspirations ("the first sources of inspiration"). The concept can be "a story with different nuances in the space, an inspirational lifestyle or even an element that connects with the brand". The team keeps working "until we are satisfied with the combinations of materials, textures, and colors" — [Interiors from Spain interview](https://www.interiorsfromspain.com/en/features/interviews/2020/masquespacio)
- Masquespacio F&B examples are product-led concepts. Novu Waffle (Riyadh) uses a waffle grid across facade, ceilings, walls and furniture. MO bakehouse (Khobar) presents baked goods "as jewels" — [Dezeen, Novu Waffle](https://www.dezeen.com/2025/04/22/masquespacio-novu-waffle-house-riyadh/amp/); [Divisare, MO](https://divisare.com/projects/465850-masquespacio-bakehouse-espresso-bar-mo)
- YOD Group (Kyiv) builds palettes extracted from context and product. For Terra the aim was to "extract colours, textures and impressions from the landscape". Rosso uses a red-to-sandy gradient from tomato sauce and aged wine. Its Behance profile is the best public source of its presentation imagery — [Dezeen, Terra](https://www.dezeen.com/?p=1861124); [Urdesignmag, Rosso](https://www.urdesignmag.com/?p=186852); [Behance YOD](https://www.behance.net/yod/info)
- India Mahdavi for Ladurée is the closest patisserie precedent. Geneva is a "garden of delights" with pastel fluted counters plus a contrasting **dark forest-green** tea lounge with a starry feel and a monochrome diamond floor. Beverly Hills is "Marie Antoinette goes to Hollywood", with brand mint plus pink. Tokyo has meringue-shaped hand-blown glass lights and a checkered marble floor — [Designboom, Geneva](https://www.designboom.com/architecture/india-mahdavi-laduree-quai-des-bergues-geneva-switzerland-12-25-2016/); [Domino](https://www.domino.com/?p=81162); [IFDM](https://ifdm.design/2017/07/14/a-feast-for-the-eyes-and-the-palate/)
- Joyce Wang studied Architecture and Materials Science at MIT and "came to interiors through materials" — [Vogue HK](https://voguehk.com/?p=168797)

### Inferences
- Synthesising these sources gives a defensible board sequence for a patisserie concept deck (one idea per board):
  1. **Cover / Concept title.** Name, one-line concept statement, logo. One hero image.
  2. **Narrative / Story.** Brand, site and emotion, in 40-80 words, with 1 hero plus 2-3 support images. This matches Sella's "emotions" and Masquespacio's "story".
  3. **Context / Site.** The historic limestone facade, street, Baku references and existing-condition photos.
  4. **Inspiration / Atmosphere.** 5-9 curated precedent images, one dominant.
  5. **Colour palette.** Dominant, secondary and accent colours with ratios, drawn from an art or product source, as Ashby and YOD do.
  6. **Materials & finishes.** A photographed flat-lay or tray (Wearstler) plus a coded legend.
  7. **Lighting concept.** Mood images plus fixture types and colour temperature.
  8. **FF&E / Furniture.** Hero pieces, seating and display vitrines.
  9. **Motif / Detail.** Logo-derived lotus line-art translated into a pattern, inlay, metalwork, handle or vitrine detail. This mirrors Masquespacio's product-as-motif waffle grid.
  10. **Spatial sequence / Plan diagram.** The narrow, deep unit read as a journey: threshold, display, counter, seating, back of house.
- The Ladurée Geneva forest-green lounge is a direct, citable precedent for SPRING's deep emerald plus a soft, luxurious patisserie palette.

### Gaps
- No published concept-deck PDFs or board templates were found for Studio Ashby, Sella Concept, Child Studio, Dimore Studio, India Mahdavi, Joyce Wang, Linehouse or YOD Group. Their decks are client-confidential. The sequence above is inferred, not documented.
- The full Wearstler Substack text (exact board list) could not be retrieved; only the summary was available.

## 2. Concrete layout rules: grids, proportions, image-to-text ratio, captions, material codes

### Takeaway
Use a modular grid with generous margins, one dominant hero image per board, a three-tier hierarchy (hero, secondary, tertiary), at most two typefaces, minimal text, and an alphanumeric code on every material that keys to a separate spec legend. Two-letter-plus-number codes (ST-01, MT-01, PT-01, GL-01) are real industry practice in drawing sets.

### Cited Findings
- The most common board mistake is lack of hierarchy. Pick the focal element first: **hero image** (a "significant portion" of the board), then secondary drawings, then tertiary diagrams and text — [Portfoliobox](https://www.portfoliobox.com/magazine/architecture-presentation-board-layout-architecture)
- Grid: set up a modular grid "e.g., 4 columns by 4 rows" with consistent gutters. "Every element must align with the grid lines". Break the grid only for drama, e.g. a full-bleed hero — [Portfoliobox](https://www.portfoliobox.com/magazine/architecture-presentation-board-layout-architecture)
- White space is "your most powerful layout tool". Keep generous margins: "Nothing looks more amateurish than text or drawings crammed against the paper's edge" — [Portfoliobox](https://www.portfoliobox.com/magazine/architecture-presentation-board-layout-architecture)
- Typography: a maximum of two font families. Title, heading and body run in descending size. Body around 14-18pt on physical boards, kept brief. Use colour as information, with one accent colour for highlights — [Portfoliobox](https://www.portfoliobox.com/magazine/architecture-presentation-board-layout-architecture)
- Material composition: lay down hero materials first (dominant floor or key wall), then secondary and accent. Arrange spatially: floor at the bottom, walls in the middle, ceiling and lighting at the top. Upholstery and metals sit grouped nearby. Use **proportional representation**: sample size should reflect dominance in the space. Layout modes are "Grid" (uniform cut samples) or "Layered Vignette" (overlapping) — [Sherer Architects](https://www.shererarch.com/material-board-for-interior-design/)
- Labelling system: give each sample a unique code (F-01 flooring, WC-01 wallcovering, P-01 paint). Put a small discreet label next to the sample and keep a **separate spec key** listing code, description, manufacturer, product/SKU, colour or finish, and size — [Sherer Architects](https://www.shererarch.com/material-board-for-interior-design/)
- One board tells one story. Present one resolved direction, with 8-12 core materials on a residential board. If there are two directions, make two complete boards — [Sherer Architects](https://www.shererarch.com/material-board-for-interior-design/)
- Education guidance: compose a **dominant / secondary / accent** palette, "not a random swatch grid". Label each sample with material, brand/grade, size, finish, standard reference and where it is used, plus a written rationale. "The mood board sells the idea; the material board builds it" — [Studio Matrx, Material Board Workshop](https://www.studiomatrx.org/students/interior-materials-construction-1/material-board-workshop)
- Real-world code conventions found in published drawing sets:
  - **ST-01** (stone; "WHITE LIMESTONE OR TRAVERTINE") and **GL-01** (glass) — [TRDI drawing set PDF](http://trdi.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/3.-2024.02.29-Architectural-Construction-Drawing.pdf)
  - **MT-01, GL-01, TB-01, ST-01** in a hotel-room joinery drawing — [Hoxton Urban hotel room PDF](https://hoxtonurban.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/HU-Hotel-Room-PDF.pdf)
  - **PT-01** "White Matte Paint Finish - RAL 9016", alongside ST-01 and MT-01, on an interior detail sheet — [Scribd detail sheet](https://www.scribd.com/document/1012491671/1718-Ab-Cw-Id-421-Typical-Powder-Room-Details-Sheet-01)
  - MT-01 Corten steel, ST-01 bluestone, MW-01 teak, STU-01 stucco — [Saratoga Springs plan set](https://lf.saratoga-springs.org/WebLink/DocView.aspx?id=598117&dbid=0&repo=SaratogaSprings)
- Hotel fabric specs should carry Martindale rating by area, cleaning and dye-lot notes — [Kothea fabric checklist](https://www.kothea.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/hotel-fabric-specification-checklist-kothea.pdf)

### Inferences
- For SPRING, use a two-letter family plus a two-digit number, consistent across deck and drawings:
  - ST (stone)
  - MT (metal)
  - WD (wood)
  - PT (paint/plaster)
  - GL (glass)
  - FB or UP (fabric/upholstery)
  - TL (tile)
  - LT (lighting)
  - LE (leather)

  ST, MT, PT, GL and TB are verified in published sets. FB and LT are **not verified as a standard** and are firm-specific.
- Digital deck proportions: 16:9 landscape (e.g. 1920×1080 or A3 landscape for print). A 12-column grid with 80-120 px outer margins (about 5-6% of width) and 16-24 px gutters is a sensible digital translation of the 4×4 rule. The hero takes about 50-66% of the board area. These numbers are inference, not sourced.
- Image-to-text ratio: aim for about 80-90% image, with text limited to a title, a 1-2 line concept caption and coded labels. This follows from the "keep it brief" and hierarchy sources; the exact ratio is not sourced.
- Image count: 1 hero plus 2-6 supporting images per board. Inspiration boards can hold up to about 9. This is inferred from the "overcrowding" warnings, with no sourced number.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source gives an exact hero-image ratio or images-per-board number for hospitality concept decks.
- No standard (e.g. CSI or NCS) mandating FB-/LT- prefixes was found. Prefixes vary by firm.

## 3. What makes a material board look professional (real samples, shadow, scale, overlap, flat-lay)?

### Takeaway
Professional boards show real or realistically rendered samples with physical depth (soft directional shadow, thickness, overlap), sized in proportion to their use in the space. They are photographed top-down with diffused side light and documented with codes. Flat coloured rectangles (CSS-style swatches) read as amateur because they carry no texture, sheen or scale.

### Cited Findings
- Sample scale matters: "A tiny 2×2 inch carpet square tells you absolutely nothing". Recommended minimums: flooring 30×30 cm; carpet 45×45; wallcovering A3; upholstery 25×25; paint 20×25 cm painted board; veneers 15×20 — [Sherer Architects](https://www.shererarch.com/material-board-for-interior-design/)
- Layered vignette: overlap samples, e.g. drape fabric over flooring next to the tile it abuts, to show textural interplay. Use neutral rigid backing (white, grey or black foam core or Gatorboard) — [Sherer Architects](https://www.shererarch.com/material-board-for-interior-design/)
- Mattoboard renders 3D "VSamples" from a library of about 50k real-world materials with "realistic light & shadow". Users can rotate and scale samples and upload their own materials (photograph "flat with no harsh shadows"). It has 100+ templates and 3D paint in 1.6k colours. Reference images can be mounted as polaroids or torn paper. Boards can be shared as 3D links — [Mattoboard blog](https://blog.mattoboard.com/blogs/6-key-features-that-will-speed-up-your-mood-board-creation); [Mattoboard](https://mattoboard.com/)
- Flat-lay photography: use diffused light from one side with a white reflector or foam board opposite. Shadows should be "soft enough to give the props a sense of depth". Avoid on-camera flash and overhead ring lights, which "flatten the scene". Keep the camera parallel on a copy stand or overhead arm, use a 35-50 mm full-frame lens, and shoot at f/8-f/11 so everything is sharp. Use raking side light to reveal fabric texture — [Shotkit](https://shotkit.com/flat-lay-photography/); [Racklify](https://racklify.com/encyclopedia/how-to-shoot-flat-lay-product-photos-for-ecommerce-lighting-composition-and-workflow/); [Burst/Shopify](https://burst.shopify.com/tips/how-to-master-flat-lay-photography-in-2020) (product-photography blogs, not design-specific)
- Wearstler emphasises seeing materials in person and mixing families (stone, metal, wood, plaster) — [MasterClass](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/kelly-wearstlers-tips-for-creating-a-vibe-tray)
- Industry claim (unverified marketing statistic): 68-75% of firms in North America and Europe use physical sample boards alongside digital ones — [Sherer Architects](https://www.shererarch.com/material-board-for-interior-design/). Treat with caution.

### Inferences
Recipe for a digital SPRING material board, built to look like a photographed flat-lay:
- **Background.** A warm off-white plaster or limestone-toned paper texture, or the emerald itself, rather than pure #FFFFFF.
- **Samples.** Each sample is a cut-out photograph of a real material or a high-resolution texture, not a flat fill. Give it visible edge thickness: stone 10-20 mm, a brass bar or fluted brass, velvet with a folded edge.
- **Shadow.** One consistent light direction, e.g. top-left. Use a soft drop shadow at 15-25% opacity, offset 4-12 px and blurred 20-40 px, all in the same direction. Stack 2-4 samples with overlap.
- **Proportion.** Size by dominance:
  - Dominant: emerald lacquer, plaster or tile, at about 40% of the board area.
  - Secondary: limestone or travertine at about 25%, warm oak or walnut at about 15%.
  - Accents: champagne brushed brass at about 10%, plus a blush or cream fabric and fluted glass.
- **Labels.** Small codes (ST-01) with hairline leader lines, set in the secondary typeface at about 9-11 px uppercase with tracking. The full legend sits in a side column.
- **Props.** Add one or two real props for narrative and scale: a cake slice, a lotus petal, a sugar-glass shard, a linen napkin.

### Gaps
- No sourced numeric shadow parameters exist. The values above are craft inference.

## 4. Mistakes that make boards look amateur

### Takeaway
The recurring amateur failures are overcrowding, no hierarchy, generic or trendy stock imagery with no local context, flat unlabelled swatches, too many options on one board, inconsistent scale, and text-heavy layouts.

### Cited Findings
- Lack of hierarchy (everything the same size), text crammed against edges, too many fonts, too much text — [Portfoliobox](https://www.portfoliobox.com/magazine/architecture-presentation-board-layout-architecture)
- Overcrowding the board with too many ideas or conflicting styles — [Algedra](https://algedra.ae/blog/creating-the-perfect-mood-board-for-interior-design-projects); [Design Cafe](https://www.designcafe.com/blog/home-interiors/interior-design-mood-board-ideas/)
- "Overly generic or trendy images that don't reflect regional context"; relying only on screens; untested colours under real lighting — [Designed Curated (Amy Kunst)](https://www.designedcurated.com/insights/interior-design-mood-board/)
- Presenting a "buffet of options" instead of one resolved direction; out-of-scale samples — [Sherer Architects](https://www.shererarch.com/material-board-for-interior-design/)
- "Random swatch grid" instead of a dominant / secondary / accent palette; unlabelled samples — [Studio Matrx](https://www.studiomatrx.org/students/interior-materials-construction-1/material-board-workshop)
- Ashby: avoid "matchy-matchy" schemes; keep an element of surprise — [Living Etc](https://www.livingetc.com/features/sophie-ashby-interview)

### Inferences
SPRING-specific don'ts:
- Flat hex-colour rectangles standing in for materials. Show #0F2A22 as lacquer, velvet or glazed zellige instead.
- Clip-art lotus. Use the brand's actual line-art, translated into a material such as an etched brass or waterjet stone inlay.
- Western stock "pastel bakery" images with no Baku reference. Include the limestone facade and Icherisheher or Baku Art Nouveau stone carving.
- More than one gold tone. Fix a single champagne-gold reference, e.g. brushed or satin brass PVD.
- Mixing rendering styles across boards.

### Gaps
- Most "mistakes" sources are designer blogs and marketing pages, not peer-reviewed work.

## 5. Digital tools used

### Cited Findings
- Mattoboard: browser-based 3D material boards with real brands' VSamples, light and shadow, templates, own-material upload, share links, and SketchUp integration — [Mattoboard blog](https://blog.mattoboard.com/blogs/6-key-features-that-will-speed-up-your-mood-board-creation)
- Morpholio Board (iOS): boards turned into presentations, colour charts, shopping lists and furniture books. Library of 1,000+ products (Knoll, Herman Miller, Porcelanosa, Artek). AR furniture. Limited export options and no Android version — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id761867957); [Design Milk](https://design-milk.com/morpholio-board-mobile-first-app-changes-interior-design-game/); [InsideA](https://insidea.com/spotlight/listing/morpholio-board/)
- InDesign or Illustrator are recommended for setting up modular grids for presentation boards — [Portfoliobox](https://www.portfoliobox.com/magazine/architecture-presentation-board-layout-architecture)

### Inferences
- The likely pro workflow:
  1. Physical tray or flat-lay, photographed, or Mattoboard for realistic material composites.
  2. InDesign or Figma for the grid-based deck (master pages, 12-column grid, paragraph styles, a coded legend component).
  3. Milanote or Pinterest-like tools only for internal image gathering, not for the client deck.
- Canva works for quick decks but tends toward template looks.

### Gaps
- No primary sources were retrieved on Milanote, Canva or Figma use by the named studios.
